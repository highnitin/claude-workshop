Iterative Refinement

Excellence rarely arrives in one shot — learning how to refine bridges the gap between close and perfect.

WHY
Perfection is a loop, not a shot
Accepting the first output — or abandoning it entirely when it's close — are both mistakes. Iteration is the lever that takes you from "good enough" to exactly right, without starting from scratch.
WHAT
Target the delta precisely
• Ask for specific, numbered adjustments

• Name exactly which section or element to change

• Describe how to change it, not just that it needs changing

• Build on what's working rather than restarting
HOW
Name exactly what to change
Acknowledge what works first. Then list 2-3 specific changes: "Shorten the second paragraph by 50%. Change the tone from formal to conversational. Add one customer success example in section three."
NOW
Your action step
Next time Claude's output is almost right, list 2-3 specific named changes instead of saying "make it better" or starting a new conversation from scratch.
Prompt Comparison — See the Difference
✕ Weak Prompt
"Make it better."
✓ Effective Prompt
"That's a good start, but please refine it further. Make the following adjustments: 1. Make the tone more casual and friendly 2. Add a specific example of how our product has helped a customer 3. Shorten the second paragraph to focus more on the benefits rather than the features"
Why it's better: The effective prompt provides specific, numbered feedback with clear direction — allowing Claude to make targeted adjustments. "Better" is subjective and Claude can't read your mind. Specific changes turn Claude into a precise editing partner rather than a guesser.
