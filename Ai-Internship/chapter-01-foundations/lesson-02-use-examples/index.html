Use Examples

A reference point is worth a thousand words of description — show Claude what good looks like.

WHY
Examples remove all ambiguity
Format, style, tone, length, and structure are almost impossible to describe perfectly in words. An example communicates all five simultaneously — eliminating the gap between what you want and what Claude produces.
WHAT
Show the target
• Provide examples of the desired output

• Attach an existing document that matches the style

• Reference previous work you've created

• If you want a specific format, show it explicitly
HOW
Attach + adapt
Include a sample email, doc, or response verbatim. Then say "Help me write a new one following the same tone and structure, but adapted to [your current situation]."
NOW
Your action step
Find one piece of existing work (email, doc, paragraph) that matches your ideal output style. Paste it into your next Claude prompt as a reference example.
Prompt Comparison — See the Difference
✕ Weak Prompt
"Write a professional email."
✓ Effective Prompt
"I need to write a professional email to a client about a project delay. Here's a similar email I've sent before: 'Dear [Client], I hope this email finds you well. I wanted to update you on the progress of [Project Name]. Unfortunately, we've encountered an unexpected issue that will delay our completion date by approximately two weeks. We're working diligently to resolve this and will keep you updated on our progress. Please let me know if you have any questions or concerns. Best regards, [Your Name]' Help me draft a new email following a similar tone and structure, but for our current situation where we're delayed by a month due to supply chain issues."
Why it's better: The effective prompt provides a concrete example of the desired style and tone, giving Claude a clear reference point. The new email will match your established voice exactly — instead of Claude guessing what "professional" means to you.
