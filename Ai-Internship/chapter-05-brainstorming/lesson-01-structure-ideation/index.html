Structure the Ideation

Unstructured brainstorming produces scattered ideas. Structure yields organized, categorized, and immediately actionable outputs.

WHY
Structure makes ideas comparable
A random list of ideas requires judgment to evaluate. A structured list with explanations, categories, and cost ranges is a decision matrix — ready to present, prioritize, and execute without additional interpretation work.
WHAT
Set the ideation parameters
• Specify the exact number of ideas you need

• Define the type and category of ideas

• Ask for a one-line explanation for each idea

• Request categorization (cost, difficulty, audience fit, impact)
HOW
The numbered idea brief
Request N ideas + one-line "how it fosters [goal]" for each + categorize by purpose + flag one low-cost option + one premium option. The result is a complete decision matrix, not a vague list.
NOW
Your action step
For your next brainstorm, add: how many ideas you need, what category tags you want applied, and "flag the best low-cost and premium option." Notice the immediate improvement in usability.
Prompt Comparison — See the Difference
✕ Weak Prompt
"Give me some team-building ideas."
✓ Effective Prompt
"We need to come up with team-building activities for our remote team of 20 people. Can you help me brainstorm by: 1. Suggesting 10 virtual team-building activities that promote collaboration 2. For each activity, briefly explain how it fosters teamwork 3. Indicate which activities are best for: a) Ice-breakers b) Improving communication c) Problem-solving skills 4. Suggest one low-cost option and one premium option."
Why it's better: Specific parameters — 10 ideas, brief explanations, three category tags, and a budget range — produce a structured output that functions as a ready-to-use decision matrix. You can pick immediately rather than spending time evaluating an undifferentiated list.
