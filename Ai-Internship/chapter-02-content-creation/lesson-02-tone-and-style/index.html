Define Tone and Style

A mismatch between brand voice and Claude's output creates rework. Define the voice upfront to get it right first time.

WHY
Voice is brand identity
Content that sounds like it could come from any company is forgettable. Tone and style are the invisible signals that tell readers who you are. Getting them right — immediately — saves editing cycles and preserves brand consistency.
WHAT
Set the voice parameters
• Describe the desired tone (professional, casual, warm, authoritative)

• State your brand's 3 key voice attributes

• Mention style guide elements if you have them

• Specify what to avoid (jargon, passive voice, buzzwords)
HOW
The brand voice briefing
State three adjectives that describe your brand voice. List the key content elements to include. Set a word count. Specify the call-to-action if needed. One paragraph of setup saves multiple rounds of revisions.
NOW
Your action step
Write three adjectives that define your brand voice right now. Add them as "Voice: [adj1], [adj2], [adj3]" to your next content prompt and compare the output to your usual results.
Prompt Comparison — See the Difference
✕ Weak Prompt
"Write a product description."
✓ Effective Prompt
"Please help me write a product description for our new ergonomic office chair. Use a professional but engaging tone. Our brand voice is friendly, innovative, and health-conscious. The description should: 1. Highlight the chair's key ergonomic features 2. Explain how these features benefit the user's health and productivity 3. Include a brief mention of the sustainable materials used 4. End with a call-to-action encouraging readers to try the chair Aim for about 200 words."
Why it's better: Clear guidance on tone ("professional but engaging"), explicit brand attributes (friendly, innovative, health-conscious), specific content elements, and a word count gives Claude everything needed to produce on-brand copy in one pass.
